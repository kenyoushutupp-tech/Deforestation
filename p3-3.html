Problem

Deforestation causes more than just the loss of trees. It leads to soil erosion, loss of biodiversity, and changes in water flow. Forests play a natural role in controlling floods because tree roots help absorb rainwater and hold the soil together. When forests are removed, the land loses its ability to soak up water, which leads to stronger and more frequent floods. The cutting of forests also increases greenhouse gas emissions, which worsens global warming. Indigenous people who depend on forests for their livelihood are displaced, while many animals lose their natural homes and become endangered. If deforestation continues, the damage to our environment could become permanent, affecting life on Earth in many ways.
